What still works when the power goes out?

Most households find out the hard way. Phones die. Flashlights are buried in a drawer with dead batteries. The fridge is a question mark. A simple checklist — and a few small upgrades — turns a blackout into a non-event.

The five-minute outage check

Walk through these in your house tonight. If you can't answer "yes" to all five, you're one storm away from a bad evening.

  • Can you get bright, safe light in every room within 10 seconds of the lights going out?
  • Do you have at least one charged backup battery that can refill a phone twice?
  • Is there a working fire blanket within reach of the kitchen stove?
  • Can you walk the perimeter of your property at night without holding a phone?
  • If the outage lasts 8 hours, will anyone in your household be uncomfortable, anxious, or unsafe?

Why standard prep fails

The classic emergency kit is a plastic tub in a closet. It works exactly twice: the day you buy it, and the day you finally throw it out unused. The problem isn't the gear, it's the friction. Candles need matches. Flashlights need fresh batteries. Generators need fuel and a place to safely run.

The fix is gear that lives in plain sight and does its job automatically — light that stays on when the grid drops, power you can hand-crank from the couch, a fire blanket that lives next to the stove instead of a closet.

A short list of things worth owning

We won't pretend this is the only list. But after a few hundred customer notes through the 2024-2025 storm season, the same four items keep coming up. Each is small, each fixes one specific failure mode, and each pays for itself the first time you need it.

  • Backup LED bulb — screws into any standard socket, behaves like a normal bulb, and stays on for hours when the power cuts. The simplest single upgrade.
  • Hand-crank solar power bank — charges from the sun, the wall, or a crank handle. Phones, radios, headlamps. No fuel, no maintenance.
  • Fire blanket — smothers a kitchen fire faster than an extinguisher, no aim required, no cleanup. Hangs near the stove. Lasts indefinitely.
  • Portable solar spotlight — charges by day, throws a wide light across an entryway or driveway at night. No wiring, no batteries to buy.

Start with the bulb

If you only do one thing this week, start with the backup bulb. It's the lowest-friction upgrade we sell: unscrew, screw in, forget about it. The next time the power goes, the room stays lit. Everything else — the power bank, the blanket, the spotlight — layers on from there.
